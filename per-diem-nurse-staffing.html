The Short Answer

What Per Diem Nurse Staffing Means for a Houston Facility

Per diem staffing puts a licensed clinician on your floor for a single shift or a short run of shifts, without adding them to your permanent schedule or asking your own staff to work mandatory overtime. Hearthstone supplies RNs, LVNs and CNAs as the core of that service, and can also staff allied healthcare and other medical roles when you need them.

What separates one per diem provider from another is mostly not the shift itself. It is who employs the clinician, who verifies the license and exclusion status, and who carries the paperwork when the shift is over. Those are the questions the rest of this page answers. For market background, see our post on what Houston facilities should know about per diem staffing.

How a Request Works

From Open Shift to Credentialed Clinician

Facilities with a Hearthstone staffing agreement send us the open shift and we handle matching and credentialing. The faster your request is complete, the faster we can work toward the 36-72 hour placement target.

A complete request names the unit and setting, date and start and end times, the role (RN, LVN or CNA), the skills the shift needs, and anything the clinician must do before they start, such as an orientation, a badge or an EHR login. Missing details are the most common reason a request takes longer to match.

What to Include in a Shift Request

  • Facility, unit and setting (hospital floor, SNF, assisted living)
  • Date, start time, end time and shift length
  • Role needed: RN, LVN or CNA (allied roles on request)
  • Skills the shift depends on, such as med pass, wound care or telemetry
  • Orientation, badge, parking and EHR access the clinician will need
  • Who to contact on arrival and who approves the timesheet
Per Diem Roles

RN, LVN and CNA Per Diem Coverage

Each role is verified against a different source, and a good per diem process checks the right one. Allied healthcare and other medical staffing are also available; ask when you submit your request.

Registered Nurses (RN)

Per diem RNs for hospital floors, skilled nursing and other settings that need RN-level assessment and oversight. See our RN staffing page for more.

  • License status checked with the Texas Board of Nursing or Nursys
  • Compact (multistate) licenses are checked against the nurse's home state
  • Unit experience matched to your request

Licensed Vocational Nurses (LVN)

Per diem LVNs for skilled nursing, assisted living and outpatient settings where LVNs carry the medication pass and treatments. See our LVN staffing page.

  • Same Texas Board of Nursing verification path as RNs
  • Facility-type experience matched to your request
  • Skills you list on the request are considered in matching

Certified Nursing Assistants (CNA)

Per diem CNAs for the call-outs that hit skilled nursing and assisted living floors hardest. See our CNA staffing page.

  • Nurse aide status checked on the Texas Nurse Aide Registry
  • ADL and transfer experience matched to your request
  • Setting preference (SNF, assisted living, hospital) noted
Choosing a Per Diem Source

Questions to Ask Any Per Diem Provider

Shift-posting apps, registries and staffing agencies all fill per diem shifts, and their terms differ. Rather than guess at any one provider's terms, here are the questions that tell you what you are buying, with Hearthstone's answers alongside. Ask the same questions of everyone you compare.

Question to ask Hearthstone Any other provider
Who employs the clinician? Hearthstone. Clinicians are W-2 employees. Ask: employee or independent contractor?
Who carries workers' compensation? Hearthstone covers its W-2 clinicians. Ask for written confirmation.
Who verifies the license? Credentialing is managed by Hearthstone. Ask what is verified, and when.
Are exclusion lists checked? Monthly OIG and SAM checks. Ask how often, and who keeps the record.

Compliance Detail

What a Facility Should Verify on Every Per Diem Shift

Licensure. The Texas Board of Nursing states that LVN, RN and APRN licensure status can be verified on the Board's website, and that Texas verifications for these license types are processed through Nursys.com. Texas is also a Nurse Licensure Compact party state, so a nurse whose home state is another compact state may practice in Texas on a multistate license; the compact ties that privilege to the home-state license, so the home-state record is the one to check. See our walkthrough on verifying a Texas nursing license.

Nurse aides. CNAs are listed on the Texas Nurse Aide Registry kept by HHSC. Under 26 Texas Administrative Code §556.9, aides are listed there with active status after completing an approved training and competency program, and nursing facilities document an aide's paid nursing services on HHSC Form 5506-NAR at renewal. A current registry listing is the first thing to confirm for a per diem CNA.

Exclusion screening. The HHS Office of Inspector General says an excluded person may not provide services payable by federal health care programs, whether they are an employee, contractor or volunteer. OIG's special advisory bulletin uses this example: if a hospital contracts with a staffing agency for temporary or per diem nurses, the hospital will be subject to overpayment liability and may be subject to civil monetary penalty liability when an excluded nurse provides payable services. OIG also notes that providers are not required by statute or regulation to check the LEIE, but says screening each month best minimizes that exposure. Hearthstone checks clinicians monthly against the OIG and SAM lists; read more in our post on OIG and SAM screening for clinicians.

Overtime. The U.S. Department of Labor's overtime fact sheet says covered nonexempt employees must receive overtime pay for hours worked over 40 in a workweek at not less than time and one-half their regular rates of pay. Whether per diem coverage costs less than overtime depends on your own pay rates and rules, so we do not publish rate comparisons here. Talk with us about your facility's situation, and see reducing nurse staffing gaps in SNFs for tactics beyond per diem.

FAQ

Per Diem Nurse Staffing: Common Questions

What is per diem nurse staffing?

Per diem nurse staffing means a clinician covers a single shift or a short run of shifts as the facility needs them, instead of joining the permanent schedule. Hearthstone provides RNs, LVNs and CNAs as our core per diem roles, and can also staff allied healthcare and other medical roles on request.

How quickly can Hearthstone provide per diem coverage?

Our placement target is 36-72 hours after a facility requests a shift. It is a target, not a guarantee. Timing depends on the role, the shift, the unit and the credentials your facility requires, and we confirm a realistic timeline when you submit the request.

Are your per diem nurses W-2 employees or independent contractors?

Hearthstone clinicians are W-2 employees of Hearthstone. They are paid weekly and are covered by Hearthstone's workers' compensation.

How do you screen clinicians before a shift?

Background screening runs through Checkr and FACIS Level 3, drug screening runs through Quest, credentialing follows a five-step process managed by Hearthstone, and clinicians are checked monthly against the OIG and SAM exclusion lists.

Do we need a long-term contract to use per diem staffing?

Per diem coverage is arranged under a staffing services agreement with Hearthstone. Contract lengths and terms depend on the coverage you need, so ask us about your situation when you request staffing. For longer needs we also offer 13-week standard contracts.

Can you cover LVN and CNA shifts at skilled nursing and assisted living facilities?

Yes. LVNs and CNAs are part of our core per diem staffing alongside RNs, and we work with hospitals, skilled nursing facilities and assisted living communities across Greater Houston.

Which exclusion lists apply when a facility uses per diem nurses?

The HHS Office of Inspector General keeps the List of Excluded Individuals/Entities (LEIE), and federal health care programs do not pay for services furnished by an excluded person. OIG's own guidance uses a hospital using a staffing agency's per diem nurses as an example of where the facility can still face overpayment liability. Hearthstone checks clinicians monthly against the OIG and SAM lists, and your compliance team may keep its own screening as well.
