<p class="lead"><strong>The short version:</strong> Federal and Texas rules tell a skilled nursing facility <em>which</em> roles must be covered and <em>when</em>: licensed nurses around the clock, a charge nurse each shift, an RN at least 8 consecutive hours a day, and a full-time RN director of nursing unless waived. They do not set a ratio of RNs to LVNs to CNAs. That mix is your call, and it is supposed to follow your residents' needs.</p>
<h2>What each license is, in the statute</h2>
<p>The Texas Occupations Code separates the two licensed levels in <a href="https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._occ._code_section_301.002" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" style="color:var(--em);font-weight:600">Section 301.002</a>. Professional nursing, the RN scope, is an act that "requires substantial specialized judgment and skill." Vocational nursing, the LVN scope, is defined as "a directed scope of nursing practice." Both exclude medical diagnosis and prescribing.</p>
<p>"Directed" is the word that matters for scheduling. The Texas Board of Nursing's <a href="https://www.bon.texas.gov/practice_scope_of_practice_lvn.asp.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" style="color:var(--em);font-weight:600">LVN scope of practice page</a> says the LVN scope "requires appropriate supervision of a registered nurse" (citing Occupations Code 301.353 and Board Position Statement 15.27), and lists other supervising providers such as APRNs, physician assistants and physicians. It describes LVN care as focused nursing care to assigned patients with predictable health care needs. The Board's position statements, not this article, are the place to check whether a specific task, such as IV therapy or a particular medication route, is within an LVN's scope.</p>
<h2>What the facility rules require by role</h2>
<p>Two sets of rules apply: the federal conditions of participation at <a href="https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-G/part-483/subpart-B/section-483.35" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" style="color:var(--em);font-weight:600">42 CFR 483.35</a> and Texas licensure rules at <a href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/26-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-554-1001" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" style="color:var(--em);font-weight:600">26 TAC 554.1001</a>. Read together, they say:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Licensed nurses (RN or LVN):</strong> sufficient numbers on a 24-hour basis. Texas also requires a licensed nurse designated as charge nurse on each shift, unless waived. Because the rule says "licensed nurse," the charge role is not limited to RNs.</li>
<li><strong>RN:</strong> at least 8 consecutive hours a day, 7 days a week, unless waived.</li>
<li><strong>Director of nursing:</strong> a registered nurse designated full-time (40 hours per week in the Texas rule), unless waived. 26 TAC 554.1001 allows the DON to serve as charge nurse only when average daily occupancy is 60 or fewer residents.</li>
<li><strong>Nurse aides (CNAs):</strong> sufficient "other nursing personnel, including nurse aides," on a 24-hour basis. A facility may not use someone as a nurse aide for more than four months on a full-time basis unless that person has completed a training and competency program or been deemed competent, and each aide needs a performance review at least every 12 months.</li>
</ul>
<p>There is a waiver path. 26 TAC 554.1004, which governs the director of nursing, addresses a waivered facility that uses an LVN to supervise nursing services: it must have an agreement with an RN who provides the LVN at least four hours of consultation in the facility each week, and that RN "must not assume director of nursing duties." The waiver conditions themselves are set by HHSC, so confirm them there rather than relying on a summary.</p>
<h2>What the rules do not say</h2>
<p>Neither rule sets a numeric ratio. 26 TAC 554.1001 ties staffing to resident assessments, care plans and the facility's population, acuity and diagnoses, and 42 CFR 483.35 points to the facility assessment at 42 CFR 483.71. CMS removed its 2024 hours-per-resident-day minimums effective February 2, 2026; we walk through that in <a href="/blog/cms-nursing-home-staffing-rule-repeal-texas" style="color:var(--em);font-weight:600">our guide to the repeal</a>. The practical result is that the RN, LVN and CNA mix on a given shift is a judgment you should be able to explain from your census and acuity, not a number you can look up.</p>
<h2>Describing a shift so the right license fills it</h2>
<p>Most mismatches start with a vague request. When you send a need to any staffing partner, include:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>The credential you need</strong> (RN, LVN or CNA), and whether the post carries charge responsibility.</li>
<li><strong>The unit, shift and start date</strong>, including weekends and holidays.</li>
<li><strong>The duties</strong> the person will be directed to perform, such as medication administration or treatments, so that the scope question is settled before the shift, not during it.</li>
<li><strong>Any supervision arrangement</strong>: which RN or other provider the LVN works under on that shift.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3>Does a Texas nursing facility need an RN on every shift?</h3>
<p>Not under the rules cited here. 26 TAC 554.1001 and 42 CFR 483.35 require a registered nurse for at least 8 consecutive hours a day, 7 days a week, and a full-time RN director of nursing, in each case unless waived. Licensed nurses, who may be RNs or LVNs, must be on duty on a 24-hour basis, and Texas requires a licensed nurse designated as charge nurse on each shift.</p>
<h3>What is the legal difference between an RN and an LVN in Texas?</h3>
<p>Texas Occupations Code 301.002 defines professional nursing (RN) as an act requiring substantial specialized judgment and skill, and vocational nursing (LVN) as a directed scope of nursing practice. The Texas Board of Nursing states that the LVN scope requires appropriate supervision, including by a registered nurse. Both scopes exclude medical diagnosis and prescribing.</p>
<h3>Is there a required ratio of RNs to LVNs to CNAs in Texas nursing homes?</h3>
<p>26 TAC 554.1001 sets no numeric staff-to-resident ratios. It requires sufficient licensed nurses and other nursing personnel, including nurse aides, on a 24-hour basis, tied to resident assessments, care plans and the facility assessment. CMS removed its 2024 hours-per-resident-day minimums effective February 2, 2026.</p>
<h3>Can an LVN serve as charge nurse or run nursing services?</h3>
<p>Texas rule 554.1001 says a facility must designate a licensed nurse as charge nurse on each shift, so the role is not limited to RNs. The director of nursing is a different matter: the rule calls for a full-time RN director unless waived, and in a waivered facility that uses an LVN to supervise nursing services, 26 TAC 554.1004 requires an agreement with an RN who provides at least four hours of consultation in the facility each week.</p>
<h3>How should a facility describe a shift need to a staffing agency?</h3>
<p>Give the credential (RN, LVN or CNA), the unit and shift, the start date, and the duties the person will be directed to perform, including medication administration, treatments and any charge responsibilities. A clear request lets the agency match the right license to the post and lets you document it against your facility assessment.</p>
